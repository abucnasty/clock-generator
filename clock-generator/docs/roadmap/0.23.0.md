# 0.23.0: faster generation, truer ranking

This is the plan for release 0.23.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.23.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.23.0 · Faster generation, truer ranking"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

Two samples take far longer to generate than the rest: the steel wagon build and the processing-unit sample with a belt export. 0.23.0 brings their generation time in line with the others. It also takes on the wake-up count where the simulator and the game part, which ranks clocks: a product finished at full output, inserters that drop into a chest, and inserters that stay enabled all period, which 0.19.0 leaves open; and a drill's active ticks, which 0.20.0 saves by clocking drills into chests.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.23.0, each with one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.23/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. Generation time

**`0.23/search-prune`**, skipping candidates that cannot win. The steel wagon build takes 35 to 43 seconds to generate, against about 2 for a typical sample, because 80 of its 83 alternatives are fixed-swing clocks that do not hold. A survey of every sample (232 jobs: the 44 samples as they are, the 44 with backed-up belts and 144 fixture jobs) tried 2,382 alternatives and listed 1,840: 599 stable, 73 that hold but do not recover and 1,168 unstable. Of the 232 selected clocks, 217 are stable. Plain arithmetic, checked on every plan, predicts 842 of the 1,168 failures and refuses no clock that holds, chains included; the rest (recovery, start phase, lane timing) need the simulator. Loss trials are 72% of generation time. The rules, in order, with what each removes and its cost in code: (1) cap fixed-swing clocks at the swings the plan found, multiples allowed: 188 plans with no clock lost (582 if 4 clocks that are never selected may go), about 4 lines fewer; (2) decide the preconditions of two strategies before their plan run: 289 wasted runs, no net code; (3) leave out `planned` (the backed-up clock on a moving belt), which never changed a selection: about 35 lines fewer; (4) leave out `uneven-output`, which wins in 2 jobs where the next-best clock costs 162 more wake-ups a minute: about 160 lines fewer. From the grenade build comes a supply walk (`0.22/grenade-bursts` in [0.22.0.md](0.22.0.md)), the arithmetic of what a shared source holds and makes by the time each hand is due. It refused no plan that holds across the samples and the chains, but moves no clock and saves little time (0.7% outside one slow sample). Here it is built as the one supply test of the hands rule, with a check on the machine side (each input against the machine's insertion limit), so that the hands rule's fixed list of placements to try can be replaced by a computed one. Done when every sample's selection is unchanged, or each change is explained and checked in the game where a scaffold exists; the count of plans removed and the generation time saved are stated; and the net code is smaller.

**`0.23/blast-select`** (#378), rerunning only the blast jobs a change can reach. Today every engine change regenerates every blast job, a change to comments alone included. Record which engine code each blast job runs, and rerun only the jobs a change can reach. Comment-only changes rerun nothing, and one generation runs at a time on the machine. Done when, on at least two real engine changes, the selective run reports exactly what a full run reports, and the count of jobs rerun is stated.

**`0.23/belt-export-time`**: generating the processing-unit sample with a belt export (`PROCESSING_UNITS_BELT_EXPORT`) takes over five minutes, far longer than any other sample (issue #101). Its period is 116,160 ticks, and none of its clocks holds the target. Done when the cause is known and the sample generates in a time in line with the others, or the reason it cannot is recorded on the issue.

### 2. Wake-ups against the game

**`0.23/w1`**, the wake-up count against the game. At full output the game counts a held product as finished when it goes into the output, which wakes the output inserter waiting on that machine; the simulator counts it when it is made. On the belted-buffer build that is 20 wake-ups a minute the simulator misses, and the scaffold test carries a temporary allowance (`wake_ups_open`) for it. Counting it the game's way also adds wake-ups in an inserter's own pickup tick on five builds, which may not be real in the game. First settle whether a pickup-tick wake is real, and record the wake-ups of agricultural science, iron bacteria cultivation and the accumulator, which no game recording has yet. Done when the simulator's wake-ups match the game's within the scaffold test's tolerance on every scaffold build with no allowance for this cause, the rule for pickup-tick wakes is decided and documented, and any change in ranking is stated per sample. Goal 2 only: output and the guarantee are not affected. A second gap goes with it: the simulator counts no wake-up for an inserter that drops into a chest of any kind, and on the lithium plate build with a rocket silo as a buffer the recorded states give 10 a minute for each of the two inserters that fill the silo (the ranking there is unchanged). Measure whether the game wakes an inserter waiting on a full container, for every container type, buffer chests included.

**`0.23/w2`**, the wake-up count with inserters enabled all period. On the logistic science inserter crafting build (#285), the backed-up clock (observed windows without the iron plate in the period, 3 output swings, 36 ticks) is exact in the game, 48 a period, and every inserter's movement agrees with the simulator's. But the game counts 8,679 wake-ups a minute against 5,104 to 5,269 simulated, by start phase, which is 67% more against a tolerance of 25%. The clock leaves five inserters enabled the whole period (plates and circuits into the inserter assembler, plates into the gear assembler); in the game an always-enabled circuit inserter wakes 1,092 a minute and a plate inserter 1,662. The likely cause is a machine finishing several crafts in one tick: the inserter assembler makes about 148 crafts a second on a half-second recipe. With the clock for two output swings (72 ticks) the figures agree within the tolerance by start phase, 476 in the game against 649 simulated. The scaffold test carries a `wake_ups_open` for it. Related: the backed-up selection costs about eight times the wake-ups of the 2-swing one (5,196 simulated, 8,679 in the game, against 649) because only the two observed-windows clocks hold backed up; look at that when the backed-up search or the ranking is next touched. Done when the cause is found and the simulator's count is within the scaffold test's tolerance, or the reason it cannot be is recorded, with no allowance left for this build. Goal 2 only.

**`0.23/drill-activity`**, a drill's active ticks in the ranking. Since 0.20.0 a drill into a chest, car, wagon or silo is enabled only for the ticks that fill it just in time; an always-on drill is active every tick, and each tick it is disabled saves its entity update. Goal 2 counts inserter wake-ups only, so it cannot see that saving, and can rank the clocked drill worse: on the steel wagon build the drill's window moves the furnaces' phase and the selected clock's inserter wake-ups go from 81.6 to 84.1 a minute. Measure in the game what an active drill tick costs against an inserter wake-up, then fold drill activity into goal 2. Done when the cost is recorded, goal 2 counts it, and any change in ranking is stated per sample.

**`0.23/drill-burst`**, a drill that fills its container in bursts, after `0.23/drill-activity`. Where a container holds many periods of demand, a drill can be enabled far less often and fill it in one burst, as on the iron plate car build: the car holds 4,000 ore against 96 a 72-tick period, and a mining tick makes about 617, so about 8 enabled ticks refill it for some 40 periods, against 1 tick every period just in time. The drill gets its own longer clock on a counter of M periods with a modulo for the main clock, as fuel clocks already have; the inserter clocks are unchanged. Choose M from the cost of a drill's active tick against a switch-on, which `0.23/drill-activity` measures: M = 1 is today's just-in-time clock. Prove it over the longer period: the steady state, every start phase of it, and that each burst lands before the container runs dry. A drill that runs dry is no backpressure loss (the ore is in the ground; an empty patch means moving the drill), so the game protocol drops the drill cut for drill-fed builds, or keeps it only as a restart check. It does not apply where a container holds less than a period's demand, as on the steel wagon build. Done when the drill-fed samples choose M by the measured costs, hold and recover in the game, and any change in ranking is stated per sample.

## Decisions before it starts

- `0.23/search-prune`: whether fixed-swing clocks are capped strictly, which loses 4 clocks that are never selected, or at the cautious cut, which loses none.
- `0.23/search-prune`: whether trying loss trials best-first is wanted; it saves about 43% of generation time but adds a "not tried" verdict to the list.
- `0.23/search-prune`: whether `planned` and `uneven-output`, clocks a player can see, may be removed.
