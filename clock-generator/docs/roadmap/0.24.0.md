# 0.24.0: lanes and recovery

This is the plan for release 0.24.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.24.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.24.0 · Lanes and recovery"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

0.19.0 leaves places where a stable or unstable verdict on a real build is not yet what the game shows: a downstream inserter on a shared moving lane, where the simulator is too optimistic; and a build with almost no spare time after an ore loss, where it is too pessimistic. 0.24.0 takes them on, with copies that share a lane and a clock the search lost.

It advances milestone M4 (stability on real cells) and milestone M5 (backpressure rule).

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.24.0 and Sidecar the next version, since `0.24/copies-lanes` adds a field to the capture; each has one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.24/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. Lanes and recovery: where the simulator and the game part

Goal 1 packages. Each starts from a build recorded in the game where the simulator's verdict was wrong or missing; the game protocol throughout is 63 baseline periods, then a cut of one input, an output block and a cut of another input or of fuel, each followed until the output is back.

**`0.24/lane-supply`**, the shared moving-lane gap. When two inserters of one unit take from one moving belt lane, the downstream one gets only what the upstream one leaves. The simulator does not model that. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts (the build the sample modelled before #281 moved it to turbo belts), the clock v0.18.0 offered holds 256 a period in the game from most starts, but never recovers: after the first ingredient cut the downstream machine falls into 3 hands of 4 (96 of 128 a period) and stays there through every later cut. The simulator calls the same clock stable: 256 a period, long run proven, recovered after one period. Its two inserters take 20 of the lane's 30 items a second (67%). Two builds that look alike by lane share do not lock in. Chemical science with a shared foundry has four lanes with two takers each on drained turbo belts, about 7% of a lane per taker: it held 256 in 63 of 63 periods and recovered from every cut, as the simulator says. A row of three copies of logistic science inserter crafting, six takers on one moving line taking two thirds of it, held 864 a period and recovered from every cut, with a hand-made clock. So lane share alone does not predict the lock-in. Done when what tells the cases apart is found; the simulator gives the game's verdict on all three kinds of build; and the rule is checked in the game at lane uses between 30% and 70%. Where the model change moves a selected clock, that clock is checked in the game. 0.19.0 ships no warning for this (#288 decided against one), so there is none to replace: the outcome is a verdict, or a refusal where the game cannot be matched. The first step is a faithful build. The sample's config cannot be built as it stands (its second machine has productivity a transport belt does not take, and its science machines need beacons it does not list), and a config's belt id can be several physical lines: a scaffold often gives each of two takers a line of its own, which does not test the gap.

**`0.24/copies-lanes`**, copies that share a lane. The schema has every copy take from the same lanes, so the summed take can exceed a lane on builds whose copies really have lanes of their own (agricultural science feeds its copies from belts of their own). In the generation battery at 0.19.0, 16 of the 83 stable moving-belt jobs take more than 30% of a lane summed over their copies; the only row of copies recorded in the game with recovery is the one above, which held at two thirds. Let a config say whether its copies share a lane, and where they do, apply the lane rule of `0.24/lane-supply` to the summed take. The lane-capacity check of #287, a warning today over the summed take, then refuses where copies share a lane. Done when the field is in the schema, the capture and the UI; the check refuses where copies share and over-subscribe, and stays silent where they do not; and no sample's result changes without a statement why. This follows `0.24/lane-supply`: a threshold taken from lane share would refuse the row of copies that holds.

**`0.24/recovery-loss`**, recovery the simulator calls lost. On metallurgic science at the speeds and lanes of the build in #283, the science foundry makes 45.19 packs a second against a target of 45, with productivity 150 and about one tick to spare a period. The target stays 45. The simulator says every clock that holds 45 does not recover, broken by the tungsten ore stopping. In the game the moving `derived` clock (320 ticks, 240 packs a period) holds and recovers from all three: 112, 0, 0, 224, then 240 after the ore cut; 1, 0, 0, 15, 272, then 240 after the output block; 240, 240, 224, then 240 after the coal cut. The backed-up `derived-subtick` clock (640 ticks, 480) does the same. A second, small gap: a machine faster than its target leaks an extra hand to the output, 0.4% here. The moving clock makes 256 instead of 240 in 4 of 63 periods; under the backed-up clock the foundry makes 481.46 a period against the simulator's 480, and 496 in 5 of 56 periods of 640. The scaffold test cannot take the build until this is settled. Done when the simulator's recovery verdict for this build matches the game's, or the reason it cannot is recorded; the extra hand is modelled or recorded as a known limit; and the build is in the scaffold test. The mismatch is the opposite of `0.24/lane-supply`'s (too pessimistic here, too optimistic there), so settle both before relaxing any recovery check.

**`0.24/lane-fill`** (#376), a belt lane with a consumption rate. In the simulator such a lane never fills: nothing takes items off it at that rate, so an export can put on it more than its consumers take, and the export check counts only a shortfall. On cell 02 (`REAL_STUPIDFATHOBBIT_CELL02`), with everything free-running, the two export inserters put 141 electronic circuits a second on a belt whose consumers take 80; the game's scaffold lets 80 through. No verdict is known to depend on it today. Done when such a lane holds up to its capacity and an inserter dropping onto a full lane waits, as in the game.

**`0.24/search-loss`**, a start-dependent exact clock. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts, v0.18.0 offered a `derived` clock that holds 256 a period in the game from three of four recorded starts and 224 from the fourth, and never recovers. Since #251 (finite output belt stock between hands) the search finds no clock that holds from a cold start: its alternatives hold 192 of 256, or run over at 269, and the game confirms they miss. The simulator still judges v0.18.0's clock stable, so the search lost it, not the judging. It is not a goal 1 clock either way. Decide whether a clock that holds exactly from the recorded start but not from every start is worth offering, labelled as such in the result (the why-not-stable summary of #288 is where the label goes). Done when it is decided; if it is offered, the label and a game run from more than one start are in.
