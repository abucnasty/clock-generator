# Game data and what is not modelled yet

This page holds what the generator reads from the game and what it refuses by name; GOALS.md holds the goals.

The generator reads the game's recipes, items, drills, resources, container slots and the rocket silo from a dump of Factorio 2.1.21 (build 87673, `resources/data-version.json`, since 0.14.0; before that an older 2.0 dump that still named the ore melting recipes `molten-iron` and `molten-copper`). Item weights, which the dump names only where a mod sets one, come from the running game of the same install (`resources/dump-item-weights.js`, since 0.20.0). 2.1 gives a recipe a list of crafting categories; the generator reads the list to tell a biochamber from an assembler in an older sidecar's export, and, since 0.20.0, to warn about a machine whose prototype cannot make its recipe. Since 0.20.0 the data also holds each crafting machine (assembling machine and furnace prototype) with its prototype type, crafting categories, burner fuel categories and base crafting speed: a machine is its `prototype`, and its simulation rule, internal to the engine, follows from them (a furnace prototype, the recycler included, the furnace rule; a burner on nutrients the biochamber rule; any other the crafting rule, as for a machine without a prototype; `src/entities/machine/machine-rule.ts`). A config's former `type` (`machine`, `furnace`, `biochamber`) is read once as the prototype of a machine of that rule that can make the recipe, the one with the highest base crafting speed (the one a player most likely built), then one that burns no fuel, then the first in the data (`src/config/migrate-machine-type.ts`); beside a `prototype` it is dropped. A machine's `quality` is a label only: its crafting speed and productivity are what the simulation reads. The game's version the data comes from is recorded beside it and printed in the header of `npm run calls`, so a call can be told from one made on other data.

A config describes one copy of a build, and `copies` is the number of identical copies, with no inserter between copies and the belts shared; it may be fractional (5.5) when the build holds a partial block, planned as that fraction of one block's rate and read as an exact fraction, so a whole number or a short decimal (`src/config/schema.ts`). A config that holds several identical copies is refused and told to describe one (`src/config/copies-validator.ts`): the smallest repeating unit is what is planned, never a block of hundreds of inserters.

What the generator does not model is refused by name, with where it is on the roadmap (`docs/ROADMAP.md`), so that a user and the config writer know whether to wait or to work around it:
- A recipe whose only products are fluids (ore melting, acid, oil cracking): follow-up F1, a fluid rate model for the drills and inserters feeding it. Fluid ingredients are dropped from a recipe and the machine is taken to be fed; whether that lets the model's foundry run faster or slower than the game's is a question of milestone M1.
- A rocket silo as a machine, crafting rocket parts and launching: phase 2 of milestone M2. Since 0.20.0 a car, a tank, a cargo wagon standing still and a rocket silo are chests (phase 1): their slots come from the game data (a cargo wagon's by its quality), a storage size on them is refused, and a rocket silo's rocket inventory holds items by their weight and its input slots its rocket part ingredients up to the insertion limit, with no craft run. A car or wagon at the end of a line fills up or runs dry, as a buffer chest does. The sidecar exports them from 0.12.0 on, and the web form adds and edits them.
- Moving trains (a wagon swapped at a station): not modelled.
- A target product that passes through more than one container on its way out of the build: refused, roadmap milestone M2 (containers). Through one container, it is taken out of that container (since 0.20.0).
- A mining drill drops into a machine or, since 0.20.0, into a chest of any type, a car, a tank, a wagon or a silo included; one dropping into a chest gets windows from the inserters that take from it. The sidecar exports a drill's chest from 0.12.0 on, and lists a drill that drops into something it does not export as left out.
- Recyclers and per-quality output: milestone M3. The recycler's recipe data (`shared_probability` per result) is in the dump and read as a result's expected amount (the slice's width); the recycler's output on the tile in front, and its per-quality tiers, are not modelled.
- Inserters other than the legendary stack inserter (since 0.14.0, `prototype` and `quality` in the config): their swing between machines and chests and their hand come from the game data by the rule in [rules-of-the-game.md](rules-of-the-game.md) (measured once); picking up from a belt is refused for every kind and quality but the legendary stack inserter, whose timing is the only one calibrated, and dropping onto a belt keeps its timing (follow-up F7); every kind waits for a full hand as the stack inserter does, although the game data gives only the stack inserter `wait_for_full_hand`. The burner inserter is refused. The sidecar exports both fields from 0.11.0 on and the web form shows them; a capture without them is asked to be recaptured.
- Inserter filters that are a blacklist (`filter_mode: "blacklist"`, exported by the sidecar from 0.11.0 on): refused; `filters` are read as the items to move. Filters switched off in the game are not exported.
- Latches and sensors switching an inserter: not modelled and not compared; such an inserter is one to clock.
- A drop onto a belt that side-loads onto another downstream is modelled as a drop onto that belt, with that belt's room. Where the merge backs up, the game moves less than the simulator (a pickup from a side-loaded belt tile is refused). The sidecar does not mark such a drop yet.

Spoilage is not simulated (follow-up F6 for spoilage-removal inserters; `0.24/l2` in [roadmap/0.24.0.md](roadmap/0.24.0.md) for what a stable verdict means for a build whose items spoil). A loss longer than an item's spoil time spoils a loop in the game whatever the clock does, so recovery verdicts from longer losses do not carry over to builds whose items spoil.

What the generator runs without its timing having been checked in the game is generated with a warning instead: a belt pickup from a lane whose `stack_size` is not 4, or with a hand other than 16 (an inserter's `stack_size`, or an item that stacks to less). Belt pickup timing is calibrated with 16-item hands on four-item belt stacks only (`src/config/pickup-calibration.ts`, [experiments](belt-pickup/experiments.md#belt-stack-sizes-and-hand-sizes)); the warning is in the log, among the clocks' insights and in the web form's Build validation, and measuring these is follow-up F7.
