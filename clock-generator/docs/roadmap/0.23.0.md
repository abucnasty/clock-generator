# 0.23.0: fewer, simpler, clearer clocks

This is the plan for release 0.23.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.23.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.23.0 · Fewer, simpler, clearer clocks"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

Three things, in this order. Clocks that can be read: a clock with fewer combinators, laid out by group, and a reason for every inserter it leaves always on. Fewer kinds of clock and less search: arithmetic that discards candidates which cannot win, a computed placement of hands in place of a fixed list to try, and a survey of which clock strategies still earn their keep; the two samples that generate far slower than the rest (the steel wagon build and the processing-unit sample with a belt export) come in line with the others. And two families of cells the generator judges loosely or not at all: builds with biochambers (agricultural science and iron bacteria cultivation), whose items spoil, and recyclers, which it refused: a recycler that puts its results into a container, such as an ore voider, gets a clock in this release.

It advances milestone M4 (stability on real cells) and starts milestone M3 (recyclers): container results, the ore voiders and results that come by small chances, such as scrap into a rocket silo, ship in this release.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.23.0, each with one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.23/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item. The sections are lettered by track; tooling comes first.

### D. Tooling

**`0.23/blast-select`** (#378), rerunning only the blast jobs a change can reach. Today every engine change regenerates every blast job, a change to comments alone included. Record which engine code each blast job runs, and rerun only the jobs a change can reach. Comment-only changes rerun nothing, and one generation runs at a time on the machine. The two blast tools that exist today (the one in the repository's scripts and a local copy with the one-generation lock) are merged into one. Done when, on at least two real engine changes, the selective run reports exactly what a full run reports, the count of jobs rerun is stated, and one blast tool is left.

### A. Readable clocks

**`0.23/deciders`**, fewer combinators in a clock. A grouped or chain clock gets one decider per distinct window. The productivity module chain's clock has 15 window deciders plus the counter and the lock: 14 inserters in chain order, almost every one on its own window, with the same item on several deciders (copper cable three times, iron plate twice). Three changes, all arithmetic with no search:

- Item merging in every clock: one decider per distinct window, serving every item and inserter that uses that window. Inserters are enabled by "Anything > 0", so a decider's item outputs are labels and one decider can serve several items.
- Shared windows as a clock of its own: the takers that the rule places together (one stage of a chain, one group of a shared source) share one window, the earliest start and the latest end any of them needs, so each still gets its hands. It is offered next to the clock with separate windows, only where it holds from every start phase, survives every loss and passes the one-tick margin check, and the usual ranking (stable first, then fewest wake-ups) picks between them. Military science selects the shared clock; the productivity chain keeps its separate windows (10 deciders) and lists the shared clock (6) as an alternative. Drills of a group already share one window.
- A Deciders column in the list of alternatives, so a reader can pick the clock with fewer combinators.

Done when every clock is built with merged items, the shared-window clock is offered where it holds, survives and passes the margin, the decider counts before and after are stated per affected sample (the grenade build, Thaeln's cell 09, the productivity chain, cell 02, logistic science with direct insertion and inserter crafting, military science, and any other the blast shows) with the wake-ups a minute and holds/survives/margin, and every sample whose selected clock changes and has a scaffold is re-recorded in the game and its controls pass. Military science has no scaffold.

**`0.23/group-layout`** (#383), the combinators of each group apart. When the grouped-hands rule splits the takers of a source into groups that take turns (the grenade build: the north machines' plate inserters in ticks 2..43, the south machines' in ticks 58..99, each group's output swing at the start of its turn), the blueprint places the deciders without showing that split. Lay out the clock's deciders by group: the combinators of one group side by side, a gap of one or more tiles between groups, and each group's combinators in the order of its turn (output swing, then inputs, then drills). Label each group's first combinator with its turn (for example "ticks 2..43") where the blueprint already carries text. Done when the grenade sample's clock shows two clearly separated groups; the wiring is unchanged (the same signals, conditions and wires), checked by the blueprint tests and once in the game on the grenade full build; and every other clock's layout is unchanged unless it has groups. The clock hashes of grouped clocks move (positions are part of the clock's identity); the blast lists them.

**`0.23/always-on-reasons`** (#393), why an inserter is always on. The clock's inserter list shows an inserter the clock does not switch as "Always on: not on the clock", with no reason. On cell 02 (`REAL_STUPIDFATHOBBIT_CELL02`) 12 of 24 inserters are always on, several at 100% on with more than 1,000 wake-ups a minute. The engine passes the reason it left each inserter unclocked and the UI names it: it is busy the whole cycle (its hands fill the whole period, so a window would never switch it off), it feeds a loop, which the clock leaves running, it is fuel, which the machine's input limit already governs, or any other reason the planner has. The wording of each reason is approved before it ships. Done when every always-on inserter in the list names its reason on every sample, and no clock changes.

### B. Arithmetic and fewer kinds of clock

**`0.23/search-prune`**, skipping candidates that cannot win, in three steps. The steel wagon build takes 35 to 43 seconds to generate, against about 2 for a typical sample, because 80 of its 83 alternatives are fixed-swing clocks that do not hold. A survey of every sample (232 jobs: the 44 samples as they are, the 44 with backed-up belts and 144 fixture jobs) tried 2,382 alternatives and listed 1,840: 599 stable, 73 that hold but do not recover and 1,168 unstable. Of the 232 selected clocks, 217 are stable. Plain arithmetic, checked on every plan, predicts 842 of the 1,168 failures and refuses no clock that holds, chains included; the rest (recovery, start phase, lane timing) need the simulator. Loss trials are 72% of generation time.

- **B1, pruning that loses no clock.** (1) Cap fixed-swing clocks at the swings the plan found, multiples allowed: 188 plans removed with no clock lost (582 if 4 clocks that are never selected may go), about 4 lines fewer. (2) Decide the preconditions of two strategies before their plan run: 289 wasted runs, no net code. The cap built is the cautious one, which loses no clock; the strict one waits for a decision. Done when every sample's selection is unchanged, the count of plans removed and the generation time saved are stated, and the net code is smaller.
- **B2, a computed placement of hands.** From the grenade build comes a supply walk, the arithmetic of what a shared source holds and makes by the time each hand is due. It refused no plan that holds across the samples and the chains, but moves no clock and saves little time (0.7% outside one slow sample). Here it is built as the one supply test of the hands rule, with a walk on the machine side (each input against the machine's insertion limit), so that the hands rule's fixed list of placements to try is replaced by a computed one. Where no grouping passes the walks (one group, or one taker, drawing more than its source holds plus makes in its window), the rule splits each taker's hands into bursts with pauses long enough for the source to refill, computed by the walk, with no search. On the grenade build bursts lose to two groups (a 2-2-1 split that lets the foundry refill holds and recovers, at 192.9 wake-ups a minute against 118.9, and is not exact on the full build in the game), but that does not make them useless for every build: no sample yet needs them, so bursts are the fallback, and they are proved first on a small test build where groups cannot hold and bursts can (for example one assembler drawing from a source with a small buffer), checked in the simulator and the game before they ship. Done when every sample's selection is unchanged, or each change is explained and checked in the game where a scaffold exists; the fixed list of placements is gone; the bursts pass on the test build in the simulator and the game; and the net code is smaller.
- **B3, a survey of which strategies earn their keep.** After B1, B2 and `0.23/deciders`, count in how many jobs each strategy is selected and what the next-best clock costs without it: `planned` (the backed-up clock on a moving belt), `uneven-output`, `derived-lean` and `planned-lean`. Before this release's other changes, `planned` never changed a selection (about 35 lines) and `uneven-output` wins in 2 jobs where the next-best clock costs 162 more wake-ups a minute (about 160 lines). Done when the list is written with the counts and the code each removal would save. No strategy is removed before a decision on that list.

**`0.23/belt-export-time`** (#101): generating the processing-unit sample with a belt export (`PROCESSING_UNITS_BELT_EXPORT`) takes over five minutes, far longer than any other sample. Its period is 116,160 ticks (348,480 with 3 output swings per cycle), and none of its five clocks holds the target; the nearest makes 34.5 of 40 a second. Why it takes this long has not been looked into. Done when the cause is known and the sample generates in a time in line with the others, or the reason it cannot is recorded on the issue.

### C. Biochambers and agricultural science

Agricultural science is a real cell that 0.19.0 can judge only with some inserters left always enabled: it leaves its egg-loop inserters always enabled, it never reaches a proven repeat, and its recovery after a supply loss is judged without spoilage, which the game has. The track first compares the simulator with the game on every biochamber build, then settles what a stable verdict means for a build whose items spoil, then clocks the egg loop.

**`0.23/bio-audit`**, the simulator against the game on biochamber recipes, on every sample whose machines are biochambers (agricultural science, iron bacteria cultivation and any other) that has a scaffold. Compare: output per period and the long-run rate against the target, and recovery after the three family cuts; how the nutrient fuel is consumed and topped up, against the simulator's fuel rule; what spoils where (machines, chests, belts, inserter hands), how fast, and what the simulator does instead, since it does not simulate spoilage; multiple outputs and productivity on biochamber recipes; and the wake-ups a minute per inserter, where agricultural science has no game recording yet. It includes #364: why the agricultural science scaffold recording no longer matches its control (inserter 11's simulated figure is 370.16 against the control's 368.00, and the 784-item period it expects no longer appears; it fails the same way before any 0.21.0 package). Done when each difference is listed with its size, its cause where found and what to change (a model change, a sample or scaffold fix, a known limit), ranked by how much it moves a verdict or a clock; the wake-up recording of agricultural science is made; and #364 is closed with the control or the engine fixed, a game recording of the selected clock deciding which.

**`0.23/l2`**, loss recovery for builds whose items spoil. The simulator has no spoilage. In the game a loss longer than an item's spoil time spoils the loop whatever the clock does (an observation, not yet recorded in the game), so the recovery verdicts of the simulator's long loss trials, which run until the build has stopped, do not carry over to such builds. Shorter losses, including the three disturbances recorded in the game, are not affected. Requiring that recovery anyway costs wake-ups: agricultural science selects its 2-swing clock (3,090 wake-ups a minute) over batched swings (2,672) and 4 swings (2,942), which fail only the recovery check. The rule is decided: a stable verdict for a build whose items spoil requires recovery only from losses shorter than the shortest spoil time in the build. Done when the generator applies the rule and any selection that changes is checked in the game. It comes before `0.23/l1`, whose recovery check depends on it.

**`0.23/l1`**, clock the egg loop. The inserters that pass eggs between agricultural science's two egg biochambers (5 and 8, 392 wake-ups a minute each) are left always enabled by the current rule for loops ([wake-ups.md](../wake-ups.md)), and so are those that take eggs into the science biochamber (9 and 10, 883 a minute each). Together the four carry 2,550 of the selected clock's 3,090 wake-ups a minute. Clocking loops can save a lot: on iron bacteria, windows on its two loop inserters held the target in the game for ten minutes at 1,587 wake-ups a minute against 4,490 for the selected clock, and ten minutes on one build was not enough to change the rule. All four inserters (5, 8, 9 and 10) are in scope, each kept on the clock only if the target holds in the game over at least 63 baseline periods and the `0.23/l2` rule passes; otherwise it stays always on, and the reason is shown (`0.23/always-on-reasons`). Done when each of the four is either on the clock, with windows that hold in the game over at least 63 periods and pass the `0.23/l2` rule, or always on with its reason recorded, and the selected clock's wake-ups drop.

### E. Recyclers

**`0.23/recyclers`** (#13), an investigation and the support it led to. Recyclers were refused ("recyclers come with roadmap milestone M3"). Cut scaffolds from saves of the recycler designs recorded in an earlier benchmark of ore-voiding builds (one with clocked recyclers, the same unclocked, one clocked so it never starves, and two with clocked waste inserters), with the cell extractor of the benchmark tools, which is a separate generic tool. Record what a recycler does in the game: the 25% return per ingredient, quality rolls, randomness, speed, and what an inserter takes from it. Then propose how the generator would model and clock one: expected values against random outputs, what "holds" means for a random output, what recovery means, and what the samples need. The investigation is done: the scaffolds are cut, the game behaviour is recorded and the proposal is written. What ships in this release is the part it settled: a recycler that places its results into a container (a car, a chest) gets a clock, the target can count the items destroyed instead of the items made (the ore voider's rate), the results that come by chance are planned at their expected amounts with room in the windows for their spread, and two ore voider cells are samples held in the game for an hour. A recipe whose results come by small chances, such as scrap, gets one window a period for each result, on the longest period its container holds at its worst moment, worked out by arithmetic, and the scrap silo is a sample held in the game for four hours on each of its clocks. An inserter that takes several results out of that container is refused: it can stop the build for good.

## Decisions before it starts

- `0.23/search-prune` B1: whether fixed-swing clocks are capped strictly, which loses 4 clocks that are never selected, or at the cautious cut, which loses none.
- `0.23/search-prune`: whether trying loss trials best-first is wanted; it saves about 43% of generation time but adds a "not tried" verdict to the list.
- `0.23/search-prune` B3: which strategies may be removed, decided from the survey's list.

## Not in this release

- Shared lanes, recovery verdicts, the wake-up count against the game, drill activity and belt drops: [0.24.0.md](0.24.0.md).
- Spoilage-removal inserters clocked from the spoil time (follow-up F6): `later/f6` in [later.md](later.md).
- A recycler that places its results into a machine, by the drill's rule (it bypasses the machine's insertion limit), and quality tiers (milestone M3): a later release.
- Removing any clock strategy before the decision on the `0.23/search-prune` B3 list.
