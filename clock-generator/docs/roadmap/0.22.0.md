# 0.22.0: faster generation, truer ranking

This is the plan for release 0.22.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.22.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.22.0 · Faster generation, truer ranking"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

Two samples take far longer to generate than the rest: the owner's steel wagon build and the processing-unit sample with a belt export. 0.22.0 brings their generation time in line with the others. It also takes on the wake-up count where the simulator and the game part, which ranks clocks: a product finished at full output, inserters that drop into a chest, and inserters that stay enabled all period, which 0.19.0 leaves open.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.22.0, each with one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.22/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. Generation time

**`0.22/search-prune`**, skipping candidates that cannot win. The owner's steel wagon build takes 35 to 43 seconds to generate, against about 2 for a typical sample, because 80 of its 83 alternatives are fixed-swing clocks that do not hold. Stop generating fixed-swing candidates once a stable planned clock exists. Done when the steel build generates in a time in line with the others and every sample's selection is unchanged.

**`0.22/belt-export-time`**: generating the processing-unit sample with a belt export (`PROCESSING_UNITS_BELT_EXPORT`) takes over five minutes, far longer than any other sample (issue #101). Its period is 116,160 ticks, and none of its clocks holds the target. Done when the cause is known and the sample generates in a time in line with the others, or the reason it cannot is recorded on the issue.

### 2. Wake-ups against the game

**`0.22/w1`**, the wake-up count against the game. At full output the game counts a held product as finished when it goes into the output, which wakes the output inserter waiting on that machine; the simulator counts it when it is made. On the belted-buffer build that is 20 wake-ups a minute the simulator misses, and the scaffold test carries a temporary allowance (`wake_ups_open`) for it. Counting it the game's way also adds wake-ups in an inserter's own pickup tick on five builds, which may not be real in the game. First settle whether a pickup-tick wake is real, and record the wake-ups of agricultural science, iron bacteria cultivation and the accumulator, which no game recording has yet. Done when the simulator's wake-ups match the game's within the scaffold test's tolerance on every scaffold build with no allowance for this cause, the rule for pickup-tick wakes is decided and documented, and any change in ranking is stated per sample. Goal 2 only: output and the guarantee are not affected. A second gap goes with it: the simulator counts no wake-up for an inserter that drops into a chest of any kind, and on the owner's lithium plate build with a rocket silo as a buffer the recorded states give 10 a minute for each of the two inserters that fill the silo (the ranking there is unchanged). Measure whether the game wakes an inserter waiting on a full container, for every container type, buffer chests included.

**`0.22/w2`**, the wake-up count with inserters enabled all period. On the owner's logistic science inserter crafting build (#285), the backed-up clock (observed windows without the iron plate in the period, 3 output swings, 36 ticks) is exact in the game, 48 a period, and every inserter's movement agrees with the simulator's. But the game counts 8,679 wake-ups a minute against 5,104 to 5,269 simulated, by start phase, which is 67% more against a tolerance of 25%. The clock leaves five inserters enabled the whole period (plates and circuits into the inserter assembler, plates into the gear assembler); in the game an always-enabled circuit inserter wakes 1,092 a minute and a plate inserter 1,662. The likely cause is a machine finishing several crafts in one tick: the inserter assembler makes about 148 crafts a second on a half-second recipe. With the clock for two output swings (72 ticks) the figures agree within the tolerance by start phase, 476 in the game against 649 simulated. The scaffold test carries a `wake_ups_open` for it. Related: the backed-up selection costs about eight times the wake-ups of the 2-swing one (5,196 simulated, 8,679 in the game, against 649) because only the two observed-windows clocks hold backed up; look at that when the backed-up search or the ranking is next touched. Done when the cause is found and the simulator's count is within the scaffold test's tolerance, or the reason it cannot be is recorded, with no allowance left for this build. Goal 2 only.
