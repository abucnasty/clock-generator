# 0.20.0: containers and real cells

This is the plan for release 0.20.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.20.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.20.0 · Containers and real cells"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

Nothing here starts before 0.19.0 is released ([0.19.0.md](0.19.0.md)), unless the owner brings containers forward.

## What the release is for

0.19.0 makes belt pickups agree with the game and refuses what it cannot model. 0.20.0 adds containers, then takes on real cells that 0.19.0 can judge only on samples, or only with some inserters left always enabled. Agricultural science is the case in point: it leaves its egg-loop inserters always enabled, it never reaches a proven repeat, and its recovery after a supply loss is judged without spoilage, which the game has.

0.19.0 also leaves three places where a stable or unstable verdict on a real build is not yet what the game shows: a downstream inserter on a shared moving lane, where the simulator is too optimistic; a build with almost no spare time after an ore loss, where it is too pessimistic; and the wake-up count of inserters that stay enabled all period. Section 4 takes them on.

It advances milestone M2 (containers) first, then milestone M4 (stability on real cells) and milestone M5 (backpressure rule), and follow-up F7 if it is chosen.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.20.0 and Sidecar the next version (0.12.0 unless the owner sets another), each with one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.20/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. Containers

**`0.20/containers`** delivers phase 1 of milestone M2: cars, tanks and cargo wagons as chests, holding mixed items in slots that can be filtered, with wagon slots by quality taken from the game data, and a rocket silo as a chest. The silo takes any item into its rocket inventory as far as the rocket lifts its weight, and its rocket part ingredients into its input slots up to the automated insertion limit; with a rocket part recipe of another quality, the ingredients go into the rocket inventory. No craft is run, and an inserter can take from the rocket inventory. Phase 2, a silo that crafts and launches, waits for its launch sequence and timings to be recorded in the game; whether it is then a machine or something new is open. Done when they generate and are checked in the game, then on cells farmed from real bases.

### 2. Thaeln's cell 09

**`0.20/thaeln-cell09`**: a plan and a clock that support Thaeln's build (`REAL_THAELN_CELL09`) through the generator's general rules. Its coal inserter moves 15/8 hands a cycle, which stretches every planned clock to eight cycles (1,344 ticks). None of the clocks planned for it is stable, so the generator falls back to the observed-windows search, which takes about ten minutes, by far the slowest of any sample. It starts from a clock Thaeln made for this build that works in the game, which the owner will provide, and turns what its windows do into a planning rule. What the generator does today: all eight standard alternatives are unstable at 480 a second; planning with the coal out of the period (336 ticks) is unstable too; and the observed-windows search on that period ends at "no recovery", at about 1,155 wake-ups a minute. The owner is not convinced the generator supports this build correctly and investigates it apart from 0.19.0, which leaves it out of its checks and game runs. Done when the generator plans a clock for this build by a rule that applies to any inserter with a fractional hand count, without the observed-windows search; the clock holds in the game with recovery, and every other sample's result is unchanged or checked again in the game.

### 3. Agricultural science: spoiling builds and the egg loop

**`0.20/l2`**, loss recovery for builds whose items spoil. The simulator has no spoilage. In the game a loss longer than an item's spoil time spoils the loop whatever the clock does (the owner's observation; not yet recorded in the game), so the recovery verdicts of the simulator's long loss trials, which run until the build has stopped, do not carry over to such builds. Shorter losses, including the three disturbances recorded in the game, are not affected. Requiring that recovery anyway costs wake-ups: agricultural science selects its 2-swing clock (3,090 wake-ups a minute) over batched swings (2,672) and 4 swings (2,942), which fail only the recovery check. Done when the owner has decided what a stable verdict means for a build whose items spoil (for example, recovery only from losses shorter than the shortest spoil time) and the generator applies it; any selection that changes is checked in the game. It comes before `0.20/l1`, whose recovery check depends on it.

**`0.20/l1`**, clock the egg loop. The inserters that pass eggs between agricultural science's two egg biochambers (5 and 8, 392 wake-ups a minute each) are left always enabled by the current rule for loops ([wake-ups.md](../wake-ups.md)), and so are those that take eggs into the science biochamber (9 and 10, 883 a minute each). Together the four carry 2,550 of the selected clock's 3,090 wake-ups a minute. Clocking loops can save a lot: on iron bacteria, windows on its two loop inserters held the target in the game for ten minutes at 1,587 wake-ups a minute against 4,490 for the selected clock, and ten minutes on one build was not enough to change the rule. Inserters 9 and 10 are the owner's call (see the decisions below). Done when the inserters in scope get windows that hold the target in the game over at least 63 baseline periods and meet the loss rule `0.20/l2` sets, and the selected clock's wake-ups drop; otherwise the reason is recorded and they stay always enabled.

### 4. Lanes and recovery: where the simulator and the game part

Goal 1 packages. Each starts from a build recorded in the game where the simulator's verdict was wrong or missing; the game protocol throughout is 63 baseline periods, then a cut of one input, an output block and a cut of another input or of fuel, each followed until the output is back.

**`0.20/lane-supply`**, the shared moving-lane gap. When two inserters of one unit take from one moving belt lane, the downstream one gets only what the upstream one leaves. The simulator does not model that. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts (the build the sample modelled before #281 moved it to turbo belts), the clock v0.18.0 offered holds 256 a period in the game from most starts, but never recovers: after the first ingredient cut the downstream machine falls into 3 hands of 4 (96 of 128 a period) and stays there through every later cut. The simulator calls the same clock stable: 256 a period, long run proven, recovered after one period. Its two inserters take 20 of the lane's 30 items a second (67%). Two builds that look alike by lane share do not lock in. Chemical science with a shared foundry has four lanes with two takers each on drained turbo belts, about 7% of a lane per taker: it held 256 in 63 of 63 periods and recovered from every cut, as the simulator says. A row of three copies of logistic science inserter crafting, six takers on one moving line taking two thirds of it, held 864 a period and recovered from every cut, with the owner's own clock. So lane share alone does not predict the lock-in. Done when what tells the cases apart is found; the simulator gives the game's verdict on all three kinds of build; and the rule is checked in the game at lane uses between 30% and 70%. Where the model change moves a selected clock, that clock is checked in the game. 0.19.0 ships no warning for this (the owner decided against one with #288), so there is none to replace: the outcome is a verdict, or a refusal where the game cannot be matched. The first step is a faithful build. The sample's config cannot be built as it stands (its second machine has productivity a transport belt does not take, and its science machines need beacons it does not list), and a config's belt id can be several physical lines: an owner scaffold often gives each of two takers a line of its own, which does not test the gap.

**`0.20/copies-lanes`**, copies that share a lane. The schema has every copy take from the same lanes, so the summed take can exceed a lane on builds whose copies really have lanes of their own (agricultural science feeds its copies from belts of their own). In the generation battery at 0.19.0, 16 of the 83 stable moving-belt jobs take more than 30% of a lane summed over their copies; the only row of copies recorded in the game with recovery is the one above, which held at two thirds. Let a config say whether its copies share a lane, and where they do, apply the lane rule of `0.20/lane-supply` to the summed take. The lane-capacity check of #287, a warning today over the summed take, then refuses where copies share a lane. Done when the field is in the schema, the capture and the UI; the check refuses where copies share and over-subscribe, and stays silent where they do not; and no sample's result changes without a statement why. This follows `0.20/lane-supply`: a threshold taken from lane share would refuse the row of copies that holds.

**`0.20/recovery-loss`**, recovery the simulator calls lost. On metallurgic science at the speeds and lanes of the owner's build (#283), the science foundry makes 45.19 packs a second against a target of 45, with productivity 150 and about one tick to spare a period. The target stays 45. The simulator says every clock that holds 45 does not recover, broken by the tungsten ore stopping. In the game the moving `derived` clock (320 ticks, 240 packs a period) holds and recovers from all three: 112, 0, 0, 224, then 240 after the ore cut; 1, 0, 0, 15, 272, then 240 after the output block; 240, 240, 224, then 240 after the coal cut. The backed-up `derived-subtick` clock (640 ticks, 480) does the same. A second, small gap: a machine faster than its target leaks an extra hand to the output, 0.4% here. The moving clock makes 256 instead of 240 in 4 of 63 periods; under the backed-up clock the foundry makes 481.46 a period against the simulator's 480, and 496 in 5 of 56 periods of 640. The scaffold test cannot take the build until this is settled. Done when the simulator's recovery verdict for this build matches the game's, or the reason it cannot is recorded; the extra hand is modelled or recorded as a known limit; and the build is in the scaffold test. The mismatch is the opposite of `0.20/lane-supply`'s (too pessimistic here, too optimistic there), so settle both before relaxing any recovery check.

**`0.20/search-loss`**, a start-dependent exact clock. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts, v0.18.0 offered a `derived` clock that holds 256 a period in the game from three of four recorded starts and 224 from the fourth, and never recovers. Since #251 (finite output belt stock between hands) the search finds no clock that holds from a cold start: its alternatives hold 192 of 256, or run over at 269, and the game confirms they miss. The simulator still judges v0.18.0's clock stable, so the search lost it, not the judging. It is not a goal 1 clock either way. Decide whether a clock that holds exactly from the recorded start but not from every start is worth offering, labelled as such in the result (the why-not-stable summary of #288 is where the label goes). Done when it is decided; if it is offered, the label and a game run from more than one start are in.

### 5. Wake-ups, samples and tools

**`0.20/w1`**, the wake-up count against the game. At full output the game counts a held product as finished when it goes into the output, which wakes the output inserter waiting on that machine; the simulator counts it when it is made. On the belted-buffer build that is 20 wake-ups a minute the simulator misses, and the scaffold test carries a temporary allowance (`wake_ups_open`) for it. Counting it the game's way also adds wake-ups in an inserter's own pickup tick on five builds, which may not be real in the game. First settle whether a pickup-tick wake is real, and record the wake-ups of agricultural science, iron bacteria cultivation and the accumulator, which no game recording has yet. Done when the simulator's wake-ups match the game's within the scaffold test's tolerance on every scaffold build with no allowance for this cause, the rule for pickup-tick wakes is decided and documented, and any change in ranking is stated per sample. Goal 2 only: output and the guarantee are not affected. A second gap goes with it: the simulator counts no wake-up for an inserter that drops into a chest of any kind, and on the owner's lithium plate build with a rocket silo as a buffer the recorded states give 10 a minute for each of the two inserters that fill the silo (the ranking there is unchanged). Measure whether the game wakes an inserter waiting on a full container, for every container type, buffer chests included.

**`0.20/w2`**, the wake-up count with inserters enabled all period. On the owner's logistic science inserter crafting build (#285), the backed-up clock (observed windows without the iron plate in the period, 3 output swings, 36 ticks) is exact in the game, 48 a period, and every inserter's movement agrees with the simulator's. But the game counts 8,679 wake-ups a minute against 5,104 to 5,269 simulated, by start phase, which is 67% more against a tolerance of 25%. The clock leaves five inserters enabled the whole period (plates and circuits into the inserter assembler, plates into the gear assembler); in the game an always-enabled circuit inserter wakes 1,092 a minute and a plate inserter 1,662. The likely cause is a machine finishing several crafts in one tick: the inserter assembler makes about 148 crafts a second on a half-second recipe. With the clock for two output swings (72 ticks) the figures agree within the tolerance by start phase, 476 in the game against 649 simulated. The scaffold test carries a `wake_ups_open` for it. Related: the backed-up selection costs about eight times the wake-ups of the 2-swing one (5,196 simulated, 8,679 in the game, against 649) because only the two observed-windows clocks hold backed up; look at that when the backed-up search or the ranking is next touched. Done when the cause is found and the simulator's count is within the scaffold test's tolerance, or the reason it cannot be is recorded, with no allowance left for this build. Goal 2 only.

**`0.20/machine-labels`**, telling a machine from its product in the clock's labels. A decider's description names the machines an inserter works with by their recipe icon ("Inserters 7, 10 for [item=lithium] from [recipe=lithium]"), and where a recipe shares its product's name and icon, as most intermediates do, the line shows the same picture twice. A machine in the config does not say what it is: `type` is `machine`, `furnace` or `biochamber`, a behaviour class the engine uses, and the sidecar reads the entity's name but does not export it. Add an optional `prototype` field to machines, as inserters have since 0.14.0, exported by the sidecar and shown in the labels as `[entity=…]` and in the web form as the machine's icon; a config without it falls back to the machine's id ("from machine 3"). Done when the sidecar exports it, the schema and the web form take it, the labels name the machine, and configs without it still load and label by id.

**`0.20/sideload-drop`**, a drop onto a belt that side-loads downstream. On the owner's lithium plate build with a rocket silo as a buffer, two furnaces' output inserters drop onto underground entrances whose exits side-load onto the belt that carries the other furnaces' plates. The capture records one belt with room to spare, so the simulator gives those inserters more room than the game: the 7,560-tick clock is stable in the simulator and makes 9,440 to 9,600 of 10,080 a period in the game (the selected 1,080-tick clock holds). The owner's decision: the sidecar marks such a drop at export, as it marks a pickup from a side-loaded belt tile, and the result names it as a known limit. Done when the sidecar marks it, the generator shows the mark, and the owner's build shows it.

**`0.20/stupidfathobbit-cell02`**: Stupidfathobbit's cell 02 generates. `REAL_STUPIDFATHOBBIT_CELL02`, a processing-unit cell from the owner's save (#189), is refused in 0.19.0: one inserter puts electronic circuits onto a belt for the rest of a utility science design that the config does not include. Measure that belt's circuit draw from the owner's save, give the lane that export rate (the cell's scaffold is in the repository beside its config), and take the cell off the refusal list. Done when it generates, its result is pinned, and any verdict it gets is checked against its scaffold in the game.

**`0.20/productivity-chain`**: a complete multi-machine chain sample (issue #89) in place of the productivity-module sample 0.19.0 drops (#279). That sample was an early sketch of a five-machine chain, copper cable, plastic, electronic circuits and advanced circuits into productivity modules at 40 a second, with no raw inputs and no output belt; the coverage check has refused it since #53. The replacement has its inputs, an output belt and a scaffold. Done when the sample generates, is among the samples every engine change is checked against, and its selected clock is checked in the game.

**`0.20/belt-export-time`**: generating the processing-unit sample with a belt export (`PROCESSING_UNITS_BELT_EXPORT`) takes over five minutes, far longer than any other sample (issue #101). Its period is 116,160 ticks, and none of its clocks holds the target. Done when the cause is known and the sample generates in a time in line with the others, or the reason it cannot is recorded on the issue.

**`0.20/own-clock`**, recording a clock the owner built. The game harness records only a clock the generator made: it finds the clock's period from a decider described "Clock for…", and the recorder refuses a clock that counts from 0 ("Recorded clock value 0", `src/verification/recording.ts`). An owner-built clock on the logistic science inserter crafting build (a counter of 215, so a period of 216 ticks; one window pair for the ore and plate inserters and one for the gears; circuit and output inserters always on) had to be measured with a probe of its own: three copies on drained ore lines made 864 a period in each of 63 periods and recovered from an ore cut, an output block and a circuit cut. Done when the harness records a hand-built clock the same way as a generated one, and the owner's recorded run is a retained control beside a generated clock for the same build. It makes owner clocks comparable with generated ones directly.

### If chosen: other inserters on belts

**`0.20/f7`** delivers follow-up F7. 0.19.0 refuses a belt pickup by anything but the legendary stack inserter and warns about pickups outside 16-item hands on 4-item stacks, because belt timing is calibrated there only; a belt drop by another kind runs on the stack inserter's drop model. Measure in the game, for the kinds and qualities the owner names (by default every kind at normal and legendary quality), pickup and drop timing at all four speeds, moving and backed-up; and for the legendary stack inserter, pickups from 1–3-item stacks and with a smaller hand. Every pickup calibration and clock control so far is 16-item hands on 4-item stacks (#270 warns); the 8- and 12-item hands of the same-item captures are seeded checks only. The questions to settle in the game: with stack 3 against a hand of 16, the model takes 3+3+3+3+3+1, so does the game stop at 15; 16-grab hands at stack 1 (reach and refill); and the fixed 4-tick belt pickup in the swing period. One capture gap goes with these: a belt of unstacked items exports as 4 once belt stacking is researched, which the warning cannot see; the fix is to export the largest stack seen on the lane, without the research floor. Two capture gaps go with it: a non-stack inserter does not stack items on a belt in the game, but the model drops a stacked hand for it; and an inserter with filters on and an empty whitelist is exported like one with filters off. Done when the straight-pickup and belt-drop controls pass for each measured kind, the refusal is lifted for exactly those, and the warning is narrowed to what stays unmeasured.

## Decisions before it starts

- Follow-up F7: whether it is in this release, and which inserter kinds and qualities to calibrate.
- `0.20/l1`: whether inserters 9 and 10 are in scope with the egg loop.
- `0.20/l2`: what a stable verdict means for a build whose items spoil.
- Whether the swing-back hand that 0.19.0 records as a known limit (#276) becomes a model change in this release.
- `0.20/copies-lanes`: whether a config's copies share a lane by default, as the schema assumes today, or each have their own; and what the field is called.
- `0.20/search-loss`: whether a clock that holds exactly from the recorded start but not from every start is worth offering, labelled.

## Not in this release

- **Follow-ups for later:** follow-up F8, the capture contract, and follow-up F9, the Pickup details UI.
- **Out of scope, as in 0.19.0:** splitters as the pickup tile, and pickups from a side-loaded belt tile. A belt fed through a splitter upstream stays modelled as a plain belt unless a real build shows a wrong verdict. Shared withdrawals upstream, sparse or independently phased supply and general conveyor networks stay unsupported. Also not attempted: live inventory or arm snapshots, simulating fluids, power or circuits in general, and rebuilding beacons.
- **Known limits:** those of 0.19.0 stay as they are unless a package above reaches them. The ones in reach: spoilage (`0.20/l2`), the wake-up mechanisms (`0.20/w1`, `0.20/w2`), shared upstream withdrawals and a row of copies (`0.20/lane-supply`, `0.20/copies-lanes`) and belt drops by other inserter kinds (`0.20/f7`).
