# 0.25.0: graph planner

This is the plan for release 0.25.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.25.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.25.0 · Graph planner"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

The generator finds a clock by trying strategies one after another and simulating each. Much of what decides whether a clock can exist is plain arithmetic: what a machine can make, what an inserter can move, what a source holds and makes by the time a hand is due. [0.24.0](0.24.0.md) hands the planner what it needs: the facts of each component, the graph of the build and a feasibility pass. 0.25.0 is the planner that uses them, and nothing else.

The planner walks the graph from the terminal edges back and plans each branch for its share of the rate. The decisions that need care (taking turns, offsetting two machines fed by one, the supply walk, the check under every order the game may serve) are made only where edges share a node. The periods of the branches compose into one clock: one main counter, branch periods that divide it, deciders merged across branches. The whole build is still simulated to verify the result, losses are tried across shared nodes, clocks are ranked by their wake-ups as a whole, and the simulator stays the judge.

Done means: every sample's selection is equal or better in the blast, with no regression; fewer candidates are simulated than in 0.24.0 and generation takes less time; and there is less code, with strategies replaced by rules over the facts the components give. Fewer wake-ups may follow, but they are not the measure.

It advances milestone M4 (stability on real cells) and the ranking that goal 2 rests on.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.25.0, each with one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.25/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. The planner

**`0.25/planner`**, the traversal planner. It plans each branch of the graph for its share of the rate with the strategy that fits it, decides at shared nodes, and composes the periods into one clock. Strategies are replaced one at a time behind the blast, each replacement its own change, and each replacement is listed with what took its place. Done when every sample's selection is equal or better, the strategies that the rules replace are gone, and the net code is smaller.

### 2. Strategies it replaces

**`0.25/supply-walk`**, a computed placement of hands. The grouped hands rule tries its placements from a fixed list ([stability.md](../stability.md), "Hands in groups"). A supply walk is the arithmetic of what a shared source holds and makes by the time each hand is due. Tried on the samples and the chains it refused no plan that holds and moved no clock, and saved little time (0.7% outside one slow sample). Build it as the one supply test of the hands rule, with a walk on the machine side (each input against the machine's insertion limit), so that the fixed list of placements is replaced by a computed one. Where no grouping passes the walks (one group, or one taker, drawing more than its source holds plus makes in its window), the rule would split each taker's hands into bursts with pauses long enough for the source to refill, computed by the walk, with no search. On the grenade build bursts lose to two groups (a 2-2-1 split that lets the foundry refill holds and recovers, at 192.9 wake-ups a minute against 118.9, and is not exact on the full build in the game). No sample needs them yet, so they are the fallback, and are proved first on a small test build where groups cannot hold and bursts can (for example one assembler drawing from a source with a small buffer), in the simulator and the game. Done when every sample's selection is unchanged, or each change is explained and checked in the game where a scaffold exists; the fixed list of placements is gone; the bursts pass on the test build in the simulator and the game; and the net code is smaller.

**`0.25/strategy-survey`**, which strategies earn their keep, and a stricter cap. Count in how many jobs each remaining strategy is selected and what the next-best clock costs without it: `planned` (the backed-up clock on a moving belt) and `uneven-output`. 0.23.0 removed the `planned-lean` and `derived-lean` strategies. Before it, `planned` never changed a selection (about 35 lines) and `uneven-output` won in 2 jobs where the next-best clock costs 162 more wake-ups a minute (about 160 lines). Done when the list is written with the counts and the code each removal would save. No strategy is removed before a decision on that list. The same survey decides the cap on fixed-swing clocks: 0.23.0 tries a clock three output swings above the plan's only as a multiple of it, which loses no clock ([clock-candidates.md](../clock-candidates.md)); the survey counted 582 plans removed by a cap at the swings the plan found, against 188, and 4 clocks that are never selected lost by it.
