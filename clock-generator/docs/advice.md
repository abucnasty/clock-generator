# The rule's count and the clock-or-unwired advice

This page was split out of GOALS.md on 2026-10-15; GOALS.md holds the goals.

## The rule's count and the clock-or-unwired advice

The inserter cost series of factorio-benchmarks timed builds with and without their clocks and closed with a rule: whether a group of inserters is worth clocking follows from w, the wake-ups a minute a clock removes from each wired inserter of the group. The generator gives that call for each group as advice (since 0.11.0). The advice changes no clock, no selection and no wake-up figure the generator reports. It is about update time only, never a reason to drop a clock a build needs for goal 1.

The rule's w is not goal 2's measure ("Wake-ups, the measure for goal 2"), which the selection uses. It is counted as the series counts it, so that the generator and the series count a save the same way (`clock-generator/src/crafting/rule-count.ts`). Since 0.12.0 it is counted in true starts, version A of the series' productivity recount (`closing/evidence/productivity-recount-1.md`):
- A source wake-up is a craft finished, or a productivity payout, by the machine the inserter takes from while it waited for items (any rise of `products_finished`; D7 b).
- A sink wake-up is a craft the machine the inserter puts into truly started while the inserter waited for space, not in the tick of its own drop. A payout is not one (D7 a1). The simulator counts its starts. The game's data, a dump of the series' extractor or a recording, is read one way: the starts are worked out as the recount works them out (`clock-generator/src/crafting/true-starts.ts`), from the machine's status, products_finished, crafting speed and recipe energy. A recording also logs crafting progress, but it is rounded to 4 places and, read alone, over-reads the starts of a machine that crafts in a tick or less (2,114 too many on a plastic plant of the low density structure scaffold), so it is not used. A machine whose crafting speed or recipe is not known keeps the starts the original count infers, and is named wherever the calls are given.

How far the method can be trusted was measured against the machines' input balance (a craft consumes its item ingredients; the items dropped in the same tick are counted) on the recordings of four scaffolds (`clock-generator/scripts/true-start-accuracy.js`, 2026-10-15), with a miss line of 5 a minute or 5%. It is exact, tick for tick, on the advanced circuit, battery, accumulator and low density structure casting machines at a crafting progress a tick of 0.21 to 0.36 and +50% to +300% productivity, and on a plastic plant at 1.03. It is off by 4.5% and 6.5% on a plastic plant that crafts nearly twice a tick, and it is unreliable on biochambers (nutrients from bioflux read double; agricultural science 5.8% low unclocked; spoilage and the burning of nutrients are untested candidates). Fluid-only recipes have no yardstick. Every group the calls give names the standing of its sinks: "checked" inside those ranges, "known miss" on the last two kinds, "not checked" elsewhere (`clock-generator/src/crafting/true-start-standing.ts`).
- A pickup from a machine that no counted wake-up and no closed window in the 3 ticks before explains is counted too: the pickups of an output that takes part hands.
- An inserter that waits on a belt is never counted, nor are belts, chests and vehicles.
- The state read is the one at the start of the tick, as goal 2 reads it.

Beside it the generator keeps the series' original count (CLOSING-2), whose sink half infers a start from a finish or a turn to working, payouts included. On productive machines it counts more: on the inputs of the advanced circuit scaffold at +175%, 1,164 sink wake-ups a minute unclocked in the game against 625 true starts.

Groups are keyed as the series keys them: the kinds of the two ends, the items seen in the hand, and the recipes at the two ends. A group's w removed is its w a minute for each inserter with every inserter unwired, less its w on the clock; the pooled figure is all wake-ups removed over the inserters newly wired. The call, with the original count's beside it:

| w removed, in true starts | Call | In the original count |
|---|---|---|
| below 53.0 | leave unwired | below 63.5 |
| 53.0 to below 158.6 | leave unwired, marginal | 63.5 to below 158.6 |
| 158.6 to 175.7 | clock, marginal | 158.6 to 207.6 |
| above 175.7 | clock | above 207.6 |

The call is made in true starts because of the mechanism, not a better fit: in true starts the band is 3.31 times wide, against 3.27 in the original count, and the two held-out measurements keep their verdicts (51.28 leave unwired, 170.17 a marginal clock, both right). The thresholds in true starts were set afterwards on the same timed groups, so they are a prediction still to be judged on fresh saves, and the mechanism they rest on (D7 a1) is confirmed on two designs of one machine type, the advanced circuit electromagnetic plant; other machine types are not tested. If a payout did wake an input, the true-start count would undercount the inputs of productive machines, and the call would lean wrongly towards "leave unwired" there; groups at +0% and +100% would not change.

"Firm" means outside the band, not certain. Outside the band the call was right in every timed case of the series that showed a sign and never shown wrong; 9 timed groups outside it showed no sign, 8 of them on one utility science design. The evidence is thinner near the edges of the band, and inside it the call is the cheaper one on expected cost, placed by one held-out measurement. The rule was measured on saves of 91,000 to 606,000 entities; below that it is not known, and the generator does not know the size of the save a build goes into.

`npm run calls` gives the calls from two dumps of the series' extractor or two recordings of the recorder (one unwired, one clocked), or from a sample, whose selected clock is run in the simulator against the build with every inserter always enabled. That second run costs time, so generation never makes it. Checked before 0.12.0: on the series' own dumps (all 43 the recount counted, and the 15 pairs of its timed builds) the generator's count in true starts equals the recount on every group, and w removed agrees to rounding, including the held-out 51.28 and 170.17; the original count still equals CLOSING-2's. On the advanced circuit recordings the true starts read from progress equal the recount's, tick by tick, and so do the starts the dump method works out from the same recordings cut to what a dump holds. The simulator's count was laid against the game on four builds, the advanced circuit, accumulator, low density structure and agricultural science scaffolds, unclocked and on their selected clocks, the game read by the method (samples README). On the first three every call agreed; the pooled w removed was 9%, 3% and 42% higher in the simulator, and up to 131% for a group, most where the simulator shows an output that takes part hands as waiting on its machine and the game shows it working. On agricultural science two calls differ, in both counts: the input of bioflux and nutrients into the science biochamber, whose w removed in the game is negative (its clocked run wakes more than its unclocked run; -184 in true starts, -240 in the original count) where the simulator has 218 and 257, and with it the build's pooled call (106 against 213). Why is not known. The calls from a simulated run are checked on those four builds only.
