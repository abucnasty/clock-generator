# 0.19.0: belt pickup parity

This is the plan for release 0.19.0 while it is in flight: what the release is for, what has merged, what is left and the limits it ships with. It is dropped when 0.19.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.19.0 · Belt pickup parity"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

Inserters that pick up from belts behave as in the game at all four belt speeds (normal, fast, express, turbo) and in every pickup layout in scope, and a generated clock is called stable only where the game agrees. The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, a clock meets its target exactly and gets back to it under the same clock after a loss; goal 2, among those, the fewest wake-ups.

It advances milestone M4 (stability on real cells) for belts, and milestone M5 (backpressure rule) in part: recovery is proven by an exact repeat of the simulated state where one exists, and the clocks of the belt builds are checked in the game after three disturbances.

Engine and UI are 0.19.0 and Sidecar is 0.11.0, each with one unreleased changelog entry ([CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md)). Nothing is published until the owner releases it.

### In scope

- Belt pickups by the legendary stack inserter, with 16-item hands from 4-item belt stacks, from moving and backed-up belts.
- Pickup layouts: the straight side pickup, the underground entrance and exit from the side, the front entrance, the front exit, and over the hood of an underground exit.
- Belt drops at stack sizes 1–4.
- Startup from placement: the first hand and every hand after it, with no arrival timing taken from the game.
- Capture of all of the above in Sidecar 0.11.0, with each inserter's kind, quality and filter mode.

### Refused, with the reason

- A pickup from a belt tile that another belt side-loads onto, from one side or two merging (#259).
- A splitter as the pickup tile (#259).
- A belt pickup by any inserter but the legendary stack inserter (#260): belt timing is calibrated on it alone. Other kinds and qualities keep working on chests and machines. Measuring them is follow-up F7.
- An inserter filter set as a blacklist (#260).

### Out of scope, not refused

- Supply that passes through a splitter further upstream is modelled as a plain belt (a known limit, below). Refusing it would block captures that work today, such as bus splits.
- Belt pickups from 1–3-item stacks, or with hands other than 16, are generated with a warning (#270). Measuring them is follow-up F7.
- Thaeln's cell 09 (`REAL_THAELN_CELL09`): the owner investigates it in 0.20.0. The tests that use it stay as they are.
- A general conveyor simulator. Sparse or independently phased supply, shared withdrawals upstream, bends, other orientations and several consumers on one belt (a row of copies among them) each need their own evidence before support is claimed.

## Known limits

A clock called stable is proven only where its check reports `long_run_proven` or `start_phases_proven` (#258), and then only under the simulator's mechanics. Everywhere else the verdict rests on a sample. See also [stability.md](../stability.md), [simulator.md](../simulator.md) and [wake-ups.md](../wake-ups.md).

**Sampled, not proven:**
- Lane phases are varied one pickup at a time, never jointly and never crossed with the clock's offset.
- The default start phases are a sample; every phase is checked only with `CLOCK_PROOF_PHASES=1`.
- Loss timing is sampled on a 4×4 grid; the start-dependence starts are a sample and opt-in.
- Long runs that never repeat, such as agricultural science and metallurgic science's fractional period.

**Can break a stable verdict in the game:**
- Recovery in the game is checked with three fixed disturbances on the game-build controls only: two ingredient cuts and one output block. The simulator's loss grid, fluid losses and the real-cell scaffolds are never checked in the game.
- One cold start is modelled. Convergence from other live states is not promised; one build reaches two different steady states from different starts.
- Belt pickup slack is a rule of thumb. On a moving belt, an inserter that also takes fuel gets no extra room, so a hand of fuel uses up the slack.
- One controlled pickup supply is modelled. A row of copies, sparse supply, shared withdrawals upstream, bends, other orientations and arbitrary lane phases between pickups are unverified.
- Supply through a splitter upstream is modelled as a plain belt. When the splitter's other output takes items, moving supply arrives slower and backed-up refill can lag.
- A window that opens on a machine at its output block follows hold-up rules with unexplained exceptions. One of them (#276): when another inserter takes a machine's products off its output block in the tick a belt hand comes back from its swing, the simulator has the hand take its first stack in that tick. In the game it comes a tick later when the taker drops onto a belt (56 of 56 hands), and by the decider order when it drops into a chest (79 of 79). The game's rule, tried in the simulator, moves no selected clock. Whether the model takes it on is decided for 0.20.0.
- A fuel inserter's own clock runs with no circuit latency in the simulator, while its blueprint encodes the usual two ticks (#276). Neither has been checked in the game. Adding the two ticks changes no clock, verdict or selection, only the wake-up figures of four fuel samples, none of which has a build.
- The order of competing takers was recorded on one scaffold; that config ids follow the game's update order is assumed.
- Two inserters bringing the same item can deadlock, and no check reaches that state; the risk is read from the config.
- Fluids are not simulated: fluid ingredients are taken as fed and never cut.
- The burner energy model is checked on outcomes only (total burn against the configured rate), never tick by tick against the game.
- Spoilage is not simulated. A loss longer than an item's spoil time spoils a loop in the game whatever the clock does, so recovery verdicts from longer losses do not carry over to builds whose items spoil.
- Belt drops by other inserter kinds run on the stack inserter's drop model, uncalibrated (follow-up F7).
- A drop onto a blocked output belt is held from the tick the belt stops taking the product; the game first fills the belt behind the drop, 9 to 156 items on a ten-tile lane (#277). After the release, game and simulator agree in every period the 120 replayed recordings judge (the 16 blocked-output captures among them), and no verdict or selection depends on the instant hold; the blocked periods themselves differ.

**Skews wake-up counts and ranking only:** the sink-list wake is confirmed on one machine type, and whether it comes from the craft start or the previous finish is unknown; how an output inserter with an empty hand is woken is unknown; whether a waiting inserter is also on its other machine's list is unconfirmed; reported wake-ups are one start's count among several steady states; the simulator shows part-hand outputs waiting on their machine where the game shows them working; the cost of tracking a moving belt is not counted in goal 2.

Closing most of these is work for milestone M4 and milestone M5, beyond this release.
