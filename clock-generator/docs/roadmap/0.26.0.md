# 0.26.0: lanes, recovery and wake-ups against the game

This is the plan for release 0.26.0: what it is for, its packages in order and what it leaves for later. It is kept while the release is in flight and dropped when 0.26.0 is released. Live status is on the Real Boy board, https://github.com/users/abucnasty/projects/2, item "0.26.0 · Lanes, recovery and wake-ups against the game"; this page does not follow every merge. The milestones and follow-ups it names are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

## What the release is for

0.19.0 leaves places where a stable or unstable verdict on a real build is not yet what the game shows: a downstream inserter on a shared moving lane, where the simulator is too optimistic; and a build with almost no spare time after an ore loss, where it is too pessimistic. 0.26.0 takes them on, with copies that share a lane and a clock the search lost. It also takes on the wake-up count where the simulator and the game part, which ranks clocks: a product finished at full output, inserters that drop into a chest, and inserters that stay enabled all period, which 0.19.0 leaves open; a drill's active ticks, which 0.20.0 saves by clocking drills into chests; and the timing of a belt drop by approach, which is calibrated for one case only.

It also refines the clock of the original electromagnetic science scaffold (#100): the offset of a whole chain fed in turns, and the cost of the signals a clock sends (#437).

It advances milestone M4 (stability on real cells) and milestone M5 (backpressure rule), and the ranking that goal 2 rests on.

The goals keep their order ([GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md)): goal 1, the clock meets its target exactly and recovers; goal 2, the fewest wake-ups. Model corrections are reported apart from search-only changes, and search-only changes keep every result identical. The time spent searching at generation does not grow ([working rules](../working-rules.md), rule 6).

Engine and UI are 0.26.0 and Sidecar the next version, since `0.26/copies-lanes` adds a field to the capture; each has one unreleased changelog entry.

## Packages, in order

Packages are named `0.26/<code>`. The numbering follows the board item.

### 1. Lanes and recovery: where the simulator and the game part

Goal 1 packages. Each starts from a build recorded in the game where the simulator's verdict was wrong or missing; the game protocol throughout is 63 baseline periods, then a cut of one input, an output block and a cut of another input or of fuel, each followed until the output is back.

**`0.26/lane-supply`**, the shared moving-lane gap. When two inserters of one unit take from one moving belt lane, the downstream one gets only what the upstream one leaves. The simulator does not model that. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts (the build the sample modelled before #281 moved it to turbo belts), the clock v0.18.0 offered holds 256 a period in the game from most starts, but never recovers: after the first ingredient cut the downstream machine falls into 3 hands of 4 (96 of 128 a period) and stays there through every later cut. The simulator calls the same clock stable: 256 a period, long run proven, recovered after one period. Its two inserters take 20 of the lane's 30 items a second (67%). Two builds that look alike by lane share do not lock in. Chemical science with a shared foundry has four lanes with two takers each on drained turbo belts, about 7% of a lane per taker: it held 256 in 63 of 63 periods and recovered from every cut, as the simulator says. A row of three copies of logistic science inserter crafting, six takers on one moving line taking two thirds of it, held 864 a period and recovered from every cut, with a hand-made clock. So lane share alone does not predict the lock-in. Done when what tells the cases apart is found; the simulator gives the game's verdict on all three kinds of build; and the rule is checked in the game at lane uses between 30% and 70%. Where the model change moves a selected clock, that clock is checked in the game. 0.19.0 ships no warning for this (#288 decided against one), so there is none to replace: the outcome is a verdict, or a refusal where the game cannot be matched. The first step is a faithful build. The sample's config cannot be built as it stands (its second machine has productivity a transport belt does not take, and its science machines need beacons it does not list), and a config's belt id can be several physical lines: a scaffold often gives each of two takers a line of its own, which does not test the gap.

**`0.26/copies-lanes`**, copies that share a lane. The schema has every copy take from the same lanes, so the summed take can exceed a lane on builds whose copies really have lanes of their own (agricultural science feeds its copies from belts of their own). In the generation battery at 0.19.0, 16 of the 83 stable moving-belt jobs take more than 30% of a lane summed over their copies; the only row of copies recorded in the game with recovery is the one above, which held at two thirds. Let a config say whether its copies share a lane, and where they do, apply the lane rule of `0.26/lane-supply` to the summed take. The lane-capacity check of #287, a warning today over the summed take, then refuses where copies share a lane. Done when the field is in the schema, the capture and the UI; the check refuses where copies share and over-subscribe, and stays silent where they do not; and no sample's result changes without a statement why. This follows `0.26/lane-supply`: a threshold taken from lane share would refuse the row of copies that holds.

**`0.26/recovery-loss`**, recovery the simulator calls lost. On metallurgic science at the speeds and lanes of the build in #283, the science foundry makes 45.19 packs a second against a target of 45, with productivity 150 and about one tick to spare a period. The target stays 45. The simulator says every clock that holds 45 does not recover, broken by the tungsten ore stopping. In the game the moving `derived` clock (320 ticks, 240 packs a period) holds and recovers from all three: 112, 0, 0, 224, then 240 after the ore cut; 1, 0, 0, 15, 272, then 240 after the output block; 240, 240, 224, then 240 after the coal cut. The backed-up `derived-subtick` clock (640 ticks, 480) does the same. A second, small gap: a machine faster than its target leaks an extra hand to the output, 0.4% here. The moving clock makes 256 instead of 240 in 4 of 63 periods; under the backed-up clock the foundry makes 481.46 a period against the simulator's 480, and 496 in 5 of 56 periods of 640. The scaffold test cannot take the build until this is settled. Done when the simulator's recovery verdict for this build matches the game's, or the reason it cannot is recorded; the extra hand is modelled or recorded as a known limit; and the build is in the scaffold test. The mismatch is the opposite of `0.26/lane-supply`'s (too pessimistic here, too optimistic there), so settle both before relaxing any recovery check.

**`0.26/lane-fill`** (#376), a belt lane with a consumption rate. In the simulator such a lane never fills: nothing takes items off it at that rate, so an export can put on it more than its consumers take, and the export check counts only a shortfall. On cell 02 (`REAL_STUPIDFATHOBBIT_CELL02`), with everything free-running, the two export inserters put 141 electronic circuits a second on a belt whose consumers take 80; the game's scaffold lets 80 through. No verdict is known to depend on it today. Done when such a lane holds up to its capacity and an inserter dropping onto a full lane waits, as in the game.

**`0.26/search-loss`**, a start-dependent exact clock. On logistic science with direct insertion on yellow belts, v0.18.0 offered a `derived` clock that holds 256 a period in the game from three of four recorded starts and 224 from the fourth, and never recovers. Since #251 (finite output belt stock between hands) the search finds no clock that holds from a cold start: its alternatives hold 192 of 256, or run over at 269, and the game confirms they miss. The simulator still judges v0.18.0's clock stable, so the search lost it, not the judging. It is not a goal 1 clock either way. Decide whether a clock that holds exactly from the recorded start but not from every start is worth offering, labelled as such in the result (the why-not-stable summary of #288 is where the label goes). Done when it is decided; if it is offered, the label and a game run from more than one start are in.

### 2. Wake-ups against the game

**`0.26/w1`**, the wake-up count against the game. At full output the game counts a held product as finished when it goes into the output, which wakes the output inserter waiting on that machine; the simulator counts it when it is made. On the belted-buffer build that is 20 wake-ups a minute the simulator misses, and the scaffold test carries a temporary allowance (`wake_ups_open`) for it. Counting it the game's way also adds wake-ups in an inserter's own pickup tick on five builds, which may not be real in the game. First settle whether a pickup-tick wake is real, and record the wake-ups of iron bacteria cultivation and the accumulator, which no game recording has yet. The wake-up recording of agricultural science was made in 0.23.0 (the samples' README). Done when the simulator's wake-ups match the game's within the scaffold test's tolerance on every scaffold build with no allowance for this cause, the rule for pickup-tick wakes is decided and documented, and any change in ranking is stated per sample. Goal 2 only: output and the guarantee are not affected. A second gap goes with it: the simulator counts no wake-up for an inserter that drops into a chest of any kind, and on the lithium plate build with a rocket silo as a buffer the recorded states give 10 a minute for each of the two inserters that fill the silo (the ranking there is unchanged). Measure whether the game wakes an inserter waiting on a full container, for every container type, buffer chests included.

**`0.26/w2`**, the wake-up count with inserters enabled all period. On the logistic science inserter crafting build (#285), the backed-up clock (observed windows without the iron plate in the period, 3 output swings, 36 ticks) is exact in the game, 48 a period, and every inserter's movement agrees with the simulator's. But the game counts 8,679 wake-ups a minute against 5,104 to 5,269 simulated, by start phase, which is 67% more against a tolerance of 25%. The clock leaves five inserters enabled the whole period (plates and circuits into the inserter assembler, plates into the gear assembler); in the game an always-enabled circuit inserter wakes 1,092 a minute and a plate inserter 1,662. The likely cause is a machine finishing several crafts in one tick: the inserter assembler makes about 148 crafts a second on a half-second recipe. With the clock for two output swings (72 ticks) the figures agree within the tolerance by start phase, 476 in the game against 649 simulated. The scaffold test carries a `wake_ups_open` for it. Related: the backed-up selection costs about eight times the wake-ups of the 2-swing one (5,196 simulated, 8,679 in the game, against 649) because only the two observed-windows clocks hold backed up; look at that when the backed-up search or the ranking is next touched. Done when the cause is found and the simulator's count is within the scaffold test's tolerance, or the reason it cannot be is recorded, with no allowance left for this build. Goal 2 only.

### 3. Drills

**`0.26/drill-activity`**, a drill's active ticks in the ranking. Since 0.20.0 a drill into a chest, car, wagon or silo is enabled only for the ticks that fill it just in time; an always-on drill is active every tick, and each tick it is disabled saves its entity update. Goal 2 counts inserter wake-ups only, so it cannot see that saving, and can rank the clocked drill worse: on the steel wagon build the drill's window moves the furnaces' phase and the selected clock's inserter wake-ups go from 81.6 to 84.1 a minute. Measure in the game what an active drill tick costs against an inserter wake-up, then fold drill activity into goal 2. Done when the cost is recorded, goal 2 counts it, and any change in ranking is stated per sample.

**`0.26/drill-burst`**, a drill that fills its container in bursts, after `0.26/drill-activity`. Where a container holds many periods of demand, a drill can be enabled far less often and fill it in one burst, as on the iron plate car build: the car holds 4,000 ore against 96 a 72-tick period, and a mining tick makes about 617, so about 8 enabled ticks refill it for some 40 periods, against 1 tick every period just in time. The drill gets its own longer clock on a counter of M periods with a modulo for the main clock, as fuel clocks already have; the inserter clocks are unchanged. Choose M from the cost of a drill's active tick against a switch-on, which `0.26/drill-activity` measures: M = 1 is today's just-in-time clock. Prove it over the longer period: the steady state, every start phase of it, and that each burst lands before the container runs dry. A drill that runs dry is no backpressure loss (the ore is in the ground; an empty patch means moving the drill), so the game protocol drops the drill cut for drill-fed builds, or keeps it only as a restart check. It does not apply where a container holds less than a period's demand, as on the steel wagon build. Done when the drill-fed samples choose M by the measured costs, hold and recover in the game, and any change in ranking is stated per sample.

### 4. Belt drops

**`0.26/drop-sweep`** (#388), belt drop timing by approach. Belt drops are calibrated in the game for one case only: a turbo belt, one orientation, effective stack sizes 1 to 4 (`tests/game/mechanics/belt-drop/`, `Belt::amountToDropAtTick`). Belt pickups had a full sweep (side, front and over-the-hood approaches, every belt speed, `docs/belt-pickup/`). The tick a hand lands decides where it goes on the belt, which the drop-spacing check uses, and drops into the hood and over the hood behave differently in the game. Sweep as for pickups, measuring in the game the drop tick and each stack's landing position on the lane: into the hood (the inserter drops onto the near side of the belt it faces) and over the hood (onto the far side), with the belt running each way past the inserter, straight and at the end of a line (side-loading is the 0.21.0 mark); all four belt speeds; a receiving lane that is empty, partly occupied and full where the hand lands (how long a hand waits and where its stacks go); the stack inserter at legendary quality, and the other kinds the pickup refusal lifts for. Done when the drop timing per approach is in the simulator and documented (as `docs/belt-pickup/` is for pickups), the drop-spacing check uses each drop's landing tick by approach, its game controls pass for each measured case, and any clock or verdict that moves is checked in the game.

### 5. Hand size by signal

**`0.26/hand-size-signal`**, whole hands of a known size. Filters set by signal at a machine's output work in the game, and an inserter can then take part hands of several results. A first round found that part hands do not shorten the long periods much and cost wake-ups on today's clocks (inserter crafting: 476.4 to 900.5 a minute in the game, 96 items in every period both ways). The lead is setting the stack size by signal, which gives whole hands of a known size, onto belts too. Done when the questions that round left are decided and, if the stack size by signal is taken on, the hand size it sets is recorded in the game, modelled and checked in the game on a build whose period it shortens.

### 6. The electromagnetic science clock

**`0.26/full-chain-offset`**, the offset of a whole chain fed in turns. 0.23.0 gives the original electromagnetic science scaffold (#100) a clock in which the arms that carry supercapacitors to the science plants take turns. When machines are fed in turns, move each turned machine's other input windows with its turn too, so the whole chain follows. It is a decision at the shared node. Measure it with the generator's wake-up count and with a game count of both clocks on #100 and on #98; the diagnostic's count gives a gain of about 2.6% for one more decider. Done when it is shipped as a ranked alternative with its own proof in the game if the gain is real, or the measurement is recorded if it is not.

**`0.26/signal-cost`**, the cost of the signals a clock sends. Both clocks of #100 (with buffer chests and without) wrap every 40 ticks, so they send enable pulses about twice as often as needed and the other inserters never rest between turns, and each signal sent costs update time of its own. Count the signals sent in a minute beside the wake-ups, first as a metric the result shows, and prefer a period that lets inserters rest. Together with the full-chain offset and the open gaps of the wake-up model (section 2 above) this refines the wake-ups of the #100 clock (1,040.6 a minute with buffer chests). A benchmark of the candidate clocks in the game (unclocked, outputs only, full chain) on the benchmark tools' scaffold is proposed when the set of candidates justifies it. Done when the signals sent are counted and shown, the wake-ups of the clock are refined or the reason they cannot be is recorded, and any change in ranking is stated per sample.

## Decisions before it starts

- `0.26/copies-lanes`: whether a config's copies share a lane by default, as the schema assumes today, or each have their own; and what the field is called.
- `0.26/search-loss`: whether a clock that holds exactly from the recorded start but not from every start is worth offering, labelled.
- `0.26/hand-size-signal`: whether the stack size by signal is taken on, and whether filters by signal for several rare results on one inserter come after it.
- `0.26/signal-cost`: whether the signals sent are only shown or also decide the ranking.
